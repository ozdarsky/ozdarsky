# Coastal Wind 35 — Builder's Tips & Tricks

## 1. Before You Start

### 1.1 Workshop Requirements
- **Floor space:** minimum 14 m × 5 m (hull + working clearance all round)
- **Height:** 3.5 m minimum for hull work; 6 m if stepping mast inside
- **Temperature:** 15–25 °C for resin work. Never laminate below 12 °C.
- **Humidity:** below 70 % RH. Condensation on tooling will ruin gelcoat adhesion.
- **Ventilation:** strong forced-air exhaust ventilation during all GRP laminating.
- **Fire safety:** CO₂ extinguisher rated Class B (flammable liquids) at all times.

### 1.2 PPE — Non-Negotiable
| Operation | Required PPE |
|-----------|-------------|
| GRP laminating | Nitrile gloves, Tyvek suit, P2 + OV respirator, safety glasses |
| Sanding / grinding | Face shield (not just glasses), FFP3 dust mask, hearing protection |
| Lead keel casting | Leather gloves, leather apron, full face shield, outdoors only |
| Battery work | Insulated gloves (EV-rated), eye protection, no metal watches/jewellery |
| Electric motor | Isolate main breaker before any wiring work |

## 2. Construction Sequence (Centre-Cockpit 10.5 m GRP)

### Phase 1 — Lofting & Plugs (Weeks 1–8)
1. Prepare a flat lofting surface (concrete floor or large MDF sheets).
2. Draw the centreline and all 10 station lines (900 mm spacing) using a chalk line.
3. From the offset table, plot each station's half-breadths on both sides of centreline at each waterline height.
4. Connect plotted points with a flexible lath (3mm×50mm timber strip) pinned to fair points.
5. Trace the fair curves onto the lofting surface. This is your full-scale reference.
6. Cut 10 station frames + stem assembly + transom from 18 mm MDF.
7. Erect frames on a strongback (stiff, level, ladder-frame structure). Check for twist with diagonals.
8. Fit longitudinal stringers between frames (fore-aft battens).
9. Plank the plug with 4 mm ply or foam panels. Fill and sand to 600 grit.
10. Apply 3 coats PVA release agent then 3 coats carnauba wax before moulding.

> **Tip:** For a one-off build, use the plug directly (male mould, sacrificial). The internal GRP hull will be slightly rough inside but this is acceptable for a one-off. Sand and fair the interior after removing from the plug.

### Phase 2 — Hull Lamination (Weeks 9–18)
1. Apply gelcoat 0.6 mm wet, let reach gel stage (tacky, not liquid).
2. Surfacing tissue (100 g/m²) directly onto gelcoat.
3. Start main laminate from underwater hull upward:
   - 2× 600 g/m² CSM wet-on-wet
   - Core material (Divinycell H80) in topsides area (above WL)
   - Finish with 2× 600 g/m² CSM over core and keel root
4. Keel root FIRST: Apply full 22 mm laminate (8 layers CSM + 4 layers biaxial) to keel area before completing topsides.
5. Allow 48 h minimum before demolding.
6. Post-cure at 40 °C for 8 h (improves resin strength ~15–20 %).

> **CRITICAL:** Keel root is the highest-stress zone. Never compromise on laminate thickness here. More is better. Get this right before any other reinforcing.

### Phase 3 — Internal Structure (Weeks 19–28)
1. Remove hull from mould. Trim flash, fair interior.
2. Mark all bulkhead positions from your lofting plan.
3. Fit keel stub (solid GRP block, 300 mm deep, spanning BH-4 to BH-5):
   - This is the platform through which keel bolts pass.
   - Build up in the mould as alternating layers of GRP + thickened epoxy.
   - Drill bolt holes with a drill press guide when cured.
4. Install all bulkheads dry-first, then tab with 4× layers biaxial cloth EACH FACE.
5. Install bow thruster tunnel:
   - Cut 120 mm holes port and starboard at correct height (200 mm below DWL).
   - Bond GRP tube through hull with 6× biaxial tabbing all round.
   - Fair the tube ends flush with the hull inside and outside.
6. Install bilge stringers (GRP hat sections, longitudinal, either side of keel).
7. Install compression post (aluminium box, 100×100×5 mm) from keel stub to mast partner position.
8. Install saildrive hull opening (200 mm diameter): reinforce with 8× biaxial ring before cutting.

### Phase 4 — Deck (Weeks 29–36)
1. Build deck as a female mould or construct over deck frames.
2. Deck laminate: base + Divinycell H60 core + outer skin.
3. Reinforce deck locally (ply inserts) at: winch bases, mast partner, stanchion bases, cleats, hatch frames.
4. Cut cockpit opening and fit moulded cockpit liner or build up with GRP.
5. CENTRE COCKPIT: Cockpit floor must be minimum 300 mm above DWL (self-draining, capsize safety).
   - This gives the aft cabin interior its ceiling height — measure carefully.
   - Cockpit drain to overboard via 38 mm seacocked through-hulls at the stern.
6. Join deck to hull: hull flange + deck flange overlapping outside, bonded and bolted every 200 mm.
7. Seal hull-deck joint inside and out with polysulfide (primary seal) + GRP tape over joint outside.

### Phase 5 — Keel Casting (Weeks 37–40)
1. Build a wooden keel mould in two halves (from keel drawings).
   - The bulb shape is the most complex part — use the NACA section template from the drawings.
2. Melt lead scrap OUTDOORS in a heavy steel pot on a propane burner:
   - Lead melts at 327 °C. Use a temperature probe.
   - Skim dross with a steel ladle before pouring.
   - Have a dry-sand firebreak around the pour area.
3. Pour slowly to avoid air pockets. Tap the mould to settle lead.
4. Allow 24 h to cool. Remove the mould halves. Dress with angle grinder.
5. Drill M36 keel bolt holes using a jig (see construction details SVG).
6. Coat keel in two-part epoxy primer before fitting.

> **Alternative:** Buy a ready-made lead keel and have it machined to your fin profile. Several keel suppliers can do this from drawings. Saves weeks and is safer.

### Phase 6 — Keel Installation
1. Apply Sikaflex 291 or 3M 5200 to keel mating faces (3 mm wet layer).
2. Lift keel into position with an engine hoist. Two people required minimum.
3. Insert M36 keel bolts from below (head in keel, nut inside boat).
4. Fit large washers and backing plates inside the keel stub.
5. Tighten in cross pattern to 350 N·m. Re-torque after 24 h cure.
6. Check alignment: keel must be vertical (use plumb bob from each end of mast track).

### Phase 7 — Fitting Out (Weeks 41–60)
1. Install seacocks for all through-hulls below WL (ball valves + Marelon bodies).
2. Electric system:
   - Run main cable bus (48V DC, minimum 70 mm² wire for motor circuit).
   - Install battery bank in low, central location (below sole, near keel).
   - Install saildrive motor per manufacturer instructions.
   - Install bow thruster controller and wire to 48V bus.
   - Shore power charger (7 kW, 230V input, 48V output).
   - Solar controller (MPPT) for stern panels.
3. Plumbing: freshwater tank, grey water, bilge pump.
4. Interior joinery: build-in settees, berths, galley units.
5. Chainplates: through-bolted, bedded, sealed.
6. Mast partner ring installation.

### Phase 8 — Launch Preparation (Weeks 61–65)
1. Below-waterline barrier coat: 5–6 coats epoxy barrier (e.g. Interprotect 2000E).
2. Antifouling paint (2 coats).
3. Step the mast and tune the rig (see Rig Tips section).
4. Commission electric system — insulation resistance test, contactor check, BMS setup.
5. Sea trial: motoring first (check saildrive, thrust, bow thruster), then sailing.

## 3. Centre-Cockpit Specific Notes

### Why Centre Cockpit?
- Puts the helmsman's weight near midship → less pitch moment
- Allows large, private aft cabins well aft of the cockpit
- Stern deck free for solar arch and bathing platform
- Cockpit is lower and smaller → inherently safer (less flood volume in knockdown)

### Layout Decisions
- **Port aft cabin (owner's):** The starboard/port partition aft of the cockpit is optional. The owner's cabin can flow seamlessly into the saloon — consider a step down (150 mm) and a partial privacy curtain rather than a door. If a full door is added later, leave a 750 mm clear opening in BH-7 (aft cabin bulkhead).
- **Starboard aft (engine/toolbox):** Include a separate above-sole access hatch to the saildrive for maintenance (Oceanvolt saildrive requires annual impeller check and seal inspection). Toolbox area: min 600×400×400 mm clearance. Removable shelf/drawer system.
- **Headroom:** The keel-to-coachroof height at midship = 1,950 mm. At the aft cabins (aft of cockpit, under the stern deck), this reduces unless you raise the deck slightly. Ensure the aft berth has sitting headroom (900 mm) even if standing headroom is less near the edges.

## 4. Electric Propulsion Tips

### 4.1 Battery Installation
- **Low and central is critical for stability.** Battery weight (250 kg) should be as close to the keel as possible.
- Install batteries under the saloon sole, spanning from BH-4 to BH-5 (keel area).
- LiFePO4 prismatic cells (e.g. CATL or EVE 280Ah cells): 16S configuration for 48V nominal.
- BMS (Battery Management System): use a marine-grade BMS with isolation contactor, temperature monitoring, and remote display on chart table.
- Ventilation: LiFePO4 is much safer than older lithium chemistries but still requires ventilation (0.25 air changes/hour for the battery compartment).

### 4.2 Saildrive Service Hatch
- Install a 600×400 mm removable hatch in the engine room floor directly above the saildrive motor.
- Allows inspection and seal replacement without removing the unit.
- The rubber bellows (flexible mount between leg and hull) must be inspected annually. Failure = flooding.

### 4.3 Shore Power & Solar
- 32A 230V shore power inlet (IEC 60309 plug, blue, 3-pin) on transom or stern quarter.
- MPPT solar controller should handle 2,400 W peak from 6× 400W panels.
- Battery monitor visible at chart table: Victron BMV-712 or equivalent.

### 4.4 Regeneration Under Sail
- The Oceanvolt ServoProp 15 supports hydro-regeneration (drag prop style).
- At 6 kts boat speed: ~400 W regeneration (modest but free).
- Trade-off: feathering propeller saves more drag than regeneration gains. Use feathering mode for racing, regeneration for passage-making.

## 5. Bulb Keel Tips

### 5.1 Why a Bulb?
- Concentrates ballast at the lowest point → maximum righting moment per kg of lead.
- Reduces total draft compared to a heavy fin (you need less total keel height for same stability).
- Hydrodynamically, the bulb also generates a tip vortex that reduces induced drag (like a winglet on an aircraft).

### 5.2 Fairing the Bulb
- Bulb NACA profile must be accurate — use MDF template cut from the drawings at multiple cross-sections.
- Bulb trailing edge: radius 15 mm minimum (not sharp — cavitation risk underwater).
- Bulb-to-fin junction: must be smooth. Use epoxy fairing compound, sand progressively.
- Run a fine wire along the leading edge of the fin and bulb to check for straightness.

### 5.3 Bulb Weight Check
- Before fitting: weigh the keel on a bathroom scale or hoist scale.
- Keel should be: 3,000 kg ± 5 % (2,850–3,150 kg acceptable).
- If too light: add lead plates into cavities in the bulb before final fairing.
- If too heavy: grind away material from non-structural areas of the fin.

## 6. Rig & Sail Tips

### 6.1 Mast Rake & Balance
- Centre-cockpit boats often have split rigs (ketch or yawl) but this design is a sloop.
- Mast rake: 2–3° aft (check: hang plumb bob from masthead, it should land ~300–450 mm aft of mast step at deck level).
- For a centre-cockpit with a long aft overhang, weather helm can be pronounced. Tune by:
  - Moving mast step forward (requires slot in partner, complex)
  - Moving jib track forward
  - Using a flatter mainsail cut

### 6.2 Running Backstays
- With a tall fractional rig (forestay at 100% I), you may not need running backstays.
- If using a masthead rig (as in this design), a single fixed backstay is sufficient for most conditions.
- Add checkstays if the mast shows any forward pump on the run.

### 6.3 Sail Trim Close-Hauled
- Jib: trim to 28–32° apparent wind angle. Upper and lower telltales should stream horizontally.
- Main: traveller at centreline, ease sheet until upper batten is parallel to boom.
- In 15+ kts: activate backstay tensioner, close jib halyard ~10 mm (flattens jib top).
- Reef 1 at 22 kts TWS; Reef 2 at 28 kts TWS.

### 6.4 Reefing Points

| Reef | Main area | Jib | Conditions |
|------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| None | 44 m² | 100% | 0–20 kts |
| Reef 1 | 35 m² (−20%) | 90% | 20–25 kts |
| Reef 2 | 27 m² (−38%) | Storm jib | 25–35 kts |
| Storm | 18 m² (−60%) | 4 m² storm jib | > 35 kts |

## 7. GRP Laminating Tips

### 7.1 Corner Filleting
- All inside corners (bulkhead-to-hull, keel stub) MUST be filleted with a radiused bead of thickened resin before glass is applied.
- Minimum fillet radius: 20 mm for structural areas.
- Method: mix resin + colloidal silica to peanut butter consistency. Apply with a rounded spatula or cut-down syringe.

### 7.2 Bulkhead Tabbing
- Every bulkhead must be tabbed on BOTH faces. A single-face tab has ~40 % of the strength.
- Tab width minimum: 150 mm each side onto hull.
- Layer sequence: ×2 CSM (conforming to curves) → ×2 biaxial (load-carrying).

### 7.3 Resin Ratios
- Isophthalic polyester: 1.5–2.0 % MEKP at 20 °C. MAX 2.5 % or exotherm risk.
- Vinylester: same ratio, but always weigh — never estimate.
- Epoxy (for bonding/barrier): follow manufacturer ratio EXACTLY. Epoxy has no tolerance for off-ratio mixing.

### 7.4 Post-Cure
- For vinylester / polyester: 40 °C for 8 h after initial 24 h ambient cure.
- This significantly improves glass transition temperature and water resistance.
- A simple hot-tent (polythene sheet + electric fan heaters) works well.

## 8. Waterproofing & Sealing

### 8.1 Below Waterline (Hull)
1. Grind back to clean GRP after all structural work below WL.
2. Apply 6 coats Interprotect 2000E or equivalent epoxy barrier. Sand between coats 4 and 6.
3. Apply 2 coats ablative antifouling.

### 8.2 All Through-Hulls Below WL
- Every through-hull: seacock (ball valve) mandatory.
- Marelon (GRP) or bronze only — NOT plastic plumbing fittings.
- Bed with 3M 5200. Interior backing plate always.
- Saildrive rubber mount: inspect annually, replace every 3–5 years.
- Bow thruster tunnel ends: smooth flush fillet where tube meets hull.

### 8.3 Deck Fittings
- All bolted deck fittings: Sikaflex 291 or polysulfide sealant.
- DO NOT USE SILICONE — it prevents re-sealing and destroys paint adhesion.
- Backing plates under all load-bearing deck fittings (stanchion bases, cleats, winches).

## 9. Model Build Tips (1:11 Scale, LOA ≈ 955 mm)

### 9.1 Materials
| Component | Suggested material |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Hull | 3 mm balsa over CNC-cut frames OR foam-core (XPS) |
| Deck | 2 mm ply, shaped |
| Keel fin | 3 mm brass sheet, shaped to NACA 0010 |
| Bulb | Cast lead (fishing weights/sinkers melted in mould) |
| Mast | 12 mm carbon tube or aluminium tube |
| Sails | Rip-stop nylon or Mylar film (stiff enough for rig testing) |

### 9.2 Ballast
```
Model keel ballast: 2.25 kg lead
Lead density: 11,340 kg/m³
Required volume: 2,250 / 11,340 = 0.000198 m³ = 198 cm³
  = roughly 6×6×5.5 cm cube of lead
  Fits comfortably in the model keel fin + bulb form ✓
```

### 9.3 Testing Protocol
1. **Float test:** Mark model DWL from plans. Float in bathtub. Should sit at DWL ± 5 mm.
2. **Stability:** List the model to 30° manually. Release — it should right itself briskly.
3. **Trim:** Should float on an even keel fore-aft. Adjust ballast fore/aft if one end is low.
4. **Sailing test (pond/lake):** In light wind, should sail on a close reach without excessive leeway. A boat that makes excessive leeway has insufficient keel area or too high a CE.
5. **Document:** Note any corrections needed and apply linear scale factor ×11 for full-scale adjustments.

### 9.4 Bow Thruster in Model
- Not needed for a sailing test model. Fit a dummy tube (5 mm brass tube) to verify the tunnel location and fairing.

## 10. Anchor Locker Construction

### 10.1 Gas Bottle Separation
- CRITICAL for safety: gas bottles must not be able to leak into the bilge or cabin.
- Build a GRP partition inside the anchor locker separating gas bottle bay (starboard side) from chain well (centreline) and anchor stowage (port side).
- Gas bottle bay: drain to OVERBOARD only (not to bilge). Minimum 25 mm drain to outboard above DWL.
- Gas bottle bay lid: watertight to cabin. Can be vented to deck.
- Fit gas solenoid valve (automatic shut-off when not cooking) for safety.

### 10.2 Chain Pipe
- Windlass to be deck-mounted forward of hatch opening.
- Chain pipe: 75 mm diameter GRP or SS tube from windlass gypsy to chain well below.
- Fit chain claw / chain stopper at deck level to avoid overloading the windlass while moored.

### 10.3 Anchor Roller
- Fit stainless steel bow roller for 40 kg anchor (Rocna, Mantus, or Spade type).
- Double roller preferred: one for primary anchor, one for kedge.
- Roller bolted through bowsprit platform or stem fitting with M12 SS bolts, minimum 4 bolts.

## 11. Common Questions

**Q: Can I build this hull in cold-moulded wood instead of GRP?**  
A: Yes. Cold-moulded epoxy/wood (3–4 diagonal layers of 6 mm veneer, epoxy-laminated) produces an excellent hull. Use the same offset table and bulkhead schedule. All GRP laminate thicknesses convert to equivalent wood/epoxy. Keel attachment is identical.

**Q: How do I manage the headroom constraint (1,950 mm)?**  
A: The 1,950 mm is measured at centreline under the coachroof beam. Near the sides of the boat, headroom reduces. In the forward cabin, headroom may be less (1,750–1,800 mm) due to the deck curve. In the aft cabins under the stern deck, the maximum is constrained — ensure minimum 1,850 mm at the berth seating position.

**Q: Is 25 kWh battery enough for blue-water sailing?**  
A: For coastal and coastal-offshore sailing, yes. For extended ocean passages, consider 40–50 kWh OR a diesel generator (hidden in engine room as hybrid backup). The stern solar panels significantly extend range at anchor and in light winds.

**Q: Can I add a diesel engine as backup?**  
A: Yes. The engine compartment (starboard aft) has room for a 15 HP diesel saildrive (Volvo IPS or Yanmar SD) alongside or replacing the electric unit. A hybrid approach (electric primary + diesel generator in the toolbox area) is the most range-flexible solution.

**Q: Saildrive rubber bellows failure — how do I prevent sinking?**  
A: Install a saildrive emergency bung kit (manufacturer-supplied) and store it within 2 m of the saildrive. Practice fitting it. Inspect the bellows every season. Replace at the first sign of cracking or hardening.

**Q: Build time estimate?**  
A: 4,000–8,000 person-hours for an experienced amateur team (2 people). Solo: 6–10 years part-time, or 2–3 years full-time.
