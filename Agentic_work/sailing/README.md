# Coastal Wind 35 — Sailship Blueprint Package

## Vessel Overview

**Design name:** "Coastal Wind 35"  
**Derived from:** Revolution 29 (Scow-Bow Mini-Voyage design)  
**Type:** 9.9 m centre-cockpit GRP sloop — coastal/offshore cruiser  
**Crew:** 4–6 persons (2 double berths + settee berths)  
**Interior headroom:** 1,950 mm (clear under beams) — comfortable for a 180 cm person  

---

## Hull Design Derivation: Scow-Bow Revolution 29

The Coastal Wind 35 hull is directly derived from the **Revolution 29** scow-bow mini-voyage concept
(see [Interesting Sailboats: Revolution 29](https://interestingsailboats.blogspot.com/2018/12/revolution-29-mini-voyage-boat.html)).

### Key Scow-Bow Characteristics (borrowed from Revolution 29)

| Feature | Revolution 29 | Coastal Wind 35 |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| Bow form | Scow — plumb, flat, wide | Scow — plumb bow transom, no overhang |
| DWL half-breadth at bow | Wide flat entry | **600 mm** at FP (vs ~100 mm conventional) |
| Max beam reached at | Very early (St2–3) | **Station 2 (x=1800 mm)** — full interior width forward |
| Forward cabin | Open, wide, bright | **Open-plan berth** — seamless flow from saloon |
| LOA | 8.8 m | 9.9 m (no bow overhang) |
| Interior volume | Excellent forward | Full-width forward berth + wide saloon connection |

### Why Scow Bow?

1. **Volume forward:** The flat wide bow gives maximum interior volume in the forward cabin. A conventional bow narrows sharply forward, wasting space. The scow bow reaches close to max beam by Station 2 (x=1800 mm), giving the forward berth the same interior width as the midship saloon.
2. **Seamless saloon → forward berth:** Because the forward sections are full and wide, there is no need to step down or narrow when moving from the saloon forward. The sole level is the same throughout (no step). No dividing bulkhead is required — the entire space from x=900 mm to x=5800 mm (4,900 mm) is one open plan.
3. **Upwind performance in chop:** The scow bow's buoyancy prevents nose-diving and provides a soft, pushing entry into waves rather than slamming.
4. **Simpler construction:** Plumb bow transom = flat panel, no complex curved stem to laminate.

### Open-Plan Saloon / Forward Berth (Key Design Feature)

```
x=0        x=900       x=2800      x=5800      x=6400      x=7800     x=9300
BOW        ANCHOR      OPEN PLAN   COCKPIT     CENTRE      AFT        TRANSOM
TRANSOM    LOCKER      ZONE →      FWD ENTRY   COCKPIT     CABINS
(scow)     [watertight][fwd berth seamlessly blends into saloon — no wall]
```

- **No bulkhead** between forward berth (x=900–2800) and saloon (x=2800–5800)
- **Same sole level** throughout — the transition is truly seamless (no step)
- **Heads** (port side, x=1800–2800): separated only by a curtain or folding door — no structural wall
- **Wide double berth** forward: scow hull gives 1,570 mm interior width at x=1800 mm (vs ~900 mm conventional)
- **Visual connection:** lying in the forward berth, one can see the saloon table — the space feels like one room

### Key Design Features
- **Scow bow (Revolution 29 derived):** Wide, flat, plumb bow face — full interior width from bow to cockpit  
- **Seamless open plan:** Forward berth flows directly into saloon — no dividing wall, same sole level  
- **Bulb keel:** Classic fin keel with streamlined lead bulb at tip — high righting moment with shallow draft  
- **Centre cockpit:** Cockpit placed amidships, leaving the entire stern for aft cabins and deployable solar panels  
- **Port aft cabin:** Owner's double cabin, private aft of cockpit  
- **Starboard aft:** Engine room + toolbox compartment with direct saildrive access from above  
- **Electric propulsion:** Saildrive-mounted electric motor (recommended over shaft drive — see calculations)  
- **Bow thruster:** 120 mm tunnel thruster, electric, submerged forward  
- **Anchor locker:** Generous bow locker holding ≥ 80 m chain + propane/butane cylinders  
- **Bathing platform:** Folding/fixed platform aft of the transom, low to water  
- **Solar panel area:** Stern arch/frame above bathing platform for deployable or fixed panels

## Propulsion Decision: Saildrive vs Shaft Drive

**Recommendation: Saildrive** — reasons:
1. Compact installation — electric motor integrates directly with the saildrive leg below hull
2. No shaft, cutless bearing, or P-bracket required
3. Pod is steerable in some designs (better low-speed manoeuvring)
4. Lower noise and vibration
5. Products specifically designed for electric saildrive: **Oceanvolt ServoProp 10/15**, **Torqeedo Cruise 10.0 RS**
6. Shaft drive is still valid if the keel geometry or hull shape makes saildrive placement difficult — see construction details SVG
